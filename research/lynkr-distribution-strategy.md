## 1. The strategic decision

Lynkr's purpose is to help people make their work visible and build professional relationships that can lead to opportunities. Posts, comments, and outreach are connected ways to do that. The existing Reddit research supports concerns about generic AI language, but it does not prove demand across every audience proposed here.

The umbrella positioning is:

> **Lynkr helps you showcase your work, build meaningful connections, and open doors to opportunities on LinkedIn.**

Supporting explanation: turn your real experience into posts, thoughtful comments, and relevant outreach, with a weekly plan to keep going.

Suggested lead line: **Make your work visible. Build connections that matter.**

The shared situation is “I have work, skills, or ideas worth sharing, but I struggle to communicate them and build relationships around them.” This is a proposed message to test, not a customer quote. Keep the brand broad and make each individual asset specific to one situation.

The product’s job is broader than content. It helps a person clarify what they have done, express it in their own voice, write a relevant comment or message, and take a small weekly set of actions. The distribution should show that whole loop.

The existing Reddit research gives evidence for frustrations with applications, generic posting, and awkward networking. For the broader audience, test three connected needs: being understood for your work, connecting with relevant people, and discovering opportunities through those relationships. Jobs and referrals are examples alongside clients, collaborations, mentorship, and professional recognition.

> **Help the right people understand your work and give them a reason to connect.**

This is a positioning hypothesis. Do not promise jobs, clients, introductions, reach, or revenue. Lynkr supports communication and follow-through; it does not guarantee another person's response or operate a job board, lead marketplace, or matching network.

## 2. What is installed and how the skills fit together

The relevant marketing skills are available locally. `product-marketing` supplies positioning context, then research and channel skills use it. Skill availability is tooling context, not evidence of customer demand.

For this plan, the useful sequence is:

1. **product-marketing**: maintain the product, audience, positioning, objections, voice, proof, and goals context.
2. **customer-research**: mine public conversations and run first-party interviews. Treat Reddit as a source of language and problems, not as an advertising list.
3. **marketing-plan**: sequence the work across Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, and Revenue, with owners and measures.
4. **content-strategy**: choose pillars, searchable and shareable topics, and a source-asset system.
5. **social**: adapt the source asset to LinkedIn, Instagram, and X, then run listening and engagement routines.
6. **community-marketing**: design a useful presence in Reddit and later a member community. It is not a permission slip to promote in every thread.
7. **niche-research**: monitor current conversations and dated stories when choosing timely angles.
8. **copywriting, post-writer, post-formatter, hook-generator, reels-scripting, graphic-designer, and image**: execute individual assets after the strategy is approved.
9. **analytics, conversion-tracking, and attribution**: connect attention to signups, interviews completed, and paid activation.

The plan deliberately does not use every marketing skill. Paid ads, influencer marketing, affiliate programs, SEO at scale, and a formal community should wait until the organic message is validated.

## 3. Current product and audience context

The reviewed plans describe a ₹499/month LinkedIn co-pilot built around an interview, a private Story Vault, posts, comments, outreach, and a Calendar. Free with an account: interview, Story Vault, first win, and Calendar. Paid: the writing and outreach capabilities that fill the Calendar, with no trial (`decisions.md`, combined plan §2). Profile fix is removed. Planned capabilities must be labelled as planned until verified in the release being demonstrated.

The product’s trust boundaries are meaningful distribution material:

- Lynkr does not ask for a LinkedIn password.
- Lynkr does not scrape LinkedIn.
- Lynkr never sends messages, connection requests, or comments for the user.
- The user reviews drafts and sends messages and comments themselves. Posts publish only through the Posts plan's gated path; public claims must match the release and `LYNKR_AUTOPUBLISH` state.
- The product should never invent achievements, metrics, employers, or personal stories.

The strongest available evidence is the existing Reddit research in `app/research/reddit-pain-points.md`. It analysed 1,059 LinkedIn-centred posts after filtering a larger set and found recurring themes around recruiter silence, ghost jobs, the time cost of posting, opaque algorithms, awkward networking, spammy messages, fear of judgement, and distrust of AI slop. India-specific posts included pressure around loans and employability, tier-3 college labels, scams, and uncertainty about whether networking advice is real.

This is useful directional evidence, not a representative market survey. It leans toward job seekers and cannot establish demand from consultants, founders, or experienced professionals. Validate the broader audience with first-party conversations and observed product use; do not relabel the existing research as proof of client acquisition or business growth.

### Initial ICP hypothesis

**Shared fit:** individuals using LinkedIn who have real work, experience, skills, or ideas to share, want useful professional relationships, and need help expressing themselves and following through. Career stage and job title are secondary to this need.

**Triggers:** finishing a project without sharing it; wanting to explain expertise; looking for clients, collaborators, mentors, or a new role; meeting someone and losing touch; having useful ideas but struggling to post consistently.

**Desired outcome:** a clearer professional identity, visible evidence of work, useful relationships, and a repeatable habit of sharing and following up. Track opportunities as user-reported outcomes, not guaranteed benefits.

**Anti-persona:** people seeking automated mass outreach, fabricated credentials, guaranteed placements or clients, engagement manipulation, or a done-for-you sales agency. Users wanting to schedule approved posts can fit the gated Posts offering.

### Audience situations to test

| Audience | Situation | Demonstration | Meaningful outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working professionals and career switchers | Good work stays inside their team; expertise is hard to explain publicly | Turn a non-confidential work decision into a post and a relevant conversation | Peer connection, recognition, or career conversation |
| Freelancers and independent consultants | Their work is stronger than their public explanation of it | Explain a completed project and draft a relevant message to a potential client | Qualified enquiry or discovery conversation |
| Founders and independent builders | They build regularly but struggle to show the thinking behind the work | Share a product decision and engage with a possible collaborator | Feedback, partnership, or collaboration conversation |
| Students and freshers | Projects and learning are hard to translate into professional credibility | Explain a project and ask a specific question of a practitioner | Mentorship, referral, or opportunity conversation |

These are acquisition hypotheses, not new product modes. India's existing price and context remain the starting operational assumption; broader professional coverage does not establish international readiness or change billing.

Use a portfolio of specific examples under one promise. Begin with a repeatable situation across segments: **“I did useful work. How do I share it and connect with the right people?”** Shift distribution effort toward segments with qualified activation and repeat use rather than declaring a demographic winner in advance.

## 4. Channel roles

Each channel has one job. The same source material can travel across channels, but the user experience and call to action should change.

| Channel | Primary job | Content role | CTA | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Discovery and emotional recognition | Reels showing work-sharing, client, career, or networking moments, plus carousels | Save, share, or visit the profile link | Generic motivational clips and polished AI voiceovers |
| LinkedIn | Trust and professional intent | Founder-led stories, practical frameworks, comments, and product demonstrations | Join the interview, try Lynkr, or reply with a problem | “AI will get you hired” claims and company-only announcements |
| Reddit | Research, credibility, and qualified conversations | Helpful answers and occasional native posts | Continue the discussion; link only when context and rules allow | Drive-by links, repeated promotion, or brand-led replies |
| X | Fast feedback and network building | Short observations, build-in-public notes, threads, and replies | Follow the idea, reply, or visit the profile | Bulk replies, duplicated copy, and link-only posts |
| Owned email and site | Conversion and retention | A short weekly field note, examples, and onboarding prompts | Start or complete the interview | Sending traffic to a vague homepage with no clear next step |

Participate as a real person, disclose affiliation when relevant, read each community's rules, and avoid spam, repeated promotional replies, or engagement manipulation. These constraints follow the [Reddit Rules](https://redditinc.com/policies/reddit-rules) and [X Authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity), checked 2026-10-30. Exact communities and formats require a fit check before use.

## 5. Messaging and content pillars

Use four pillars. Every asset should fit one pillar and one audience situation. Start with 40% showcasing work, 30% useful relationships, 20% opportunities and follow-through, and 10% product trust. These are reversible editorial allocations, not research findings.

### Pillar A: Showcase your work

Make real work understandable and worth remembering. Show the problem, contribution, decision, and result without manufacturing achievements. Personal marketing is the main thread of the strategy.

Examples:

- Turn a design decision, client project, technical fix, or college project into a useful post.
- Explain what you contributed when you cannot disclose client names or metrics.
- Show what you learned from a launch that did not go as expected.

### Pillar B: Build useful relationships

Show how to participate in someone's work, ask a specific question, and keep in touch without making every interaction a pitch. Demonstrate comments and outreach together.

Examples:

- Start a conversation with someone whose work overlaps yours.
- Write a comment that adds an example from your own experience.
- Follow up after a useful conversation with a peer, mentor, or potential collaborator.

### Pillar C: Create openings for opportunities

Connect visible work to a relevant next step: a client discussion, collaboration, referral, mentorship, or career conversation. Show why that specific person and ask make sense. Avoid implying that every post must sell something.

Examples:

- Use a project example to explain why you can help with a particular problem.
- Ask a possible collaborator a question grounded in shared interests.
- Keep a promising relationship moving after the first reply.

### Pillar D: Human communication and product trust

Demonstrate how confirmed experience shapes a draft and where the user retains judgement. Use founder decisions and real research as evidence. Include examples from several professional situations.

Examples:

- Why Lynkr never asks for a LinkedIn password.
- What a real interview changed in the product.
- A feature we removed because automation would create the wrong risk.

Direct invitations should accompany relevant demonstrations. Do not let build-in-public content become the dominant message or attract only other tool builders.

## 6. The source-asset system

Use a Create Once, Distribute Twice system. Produce one substantial source asset each week, then adapt it rather than inventing separate ideas for every platform.

### Weekly source asset

Choose one of:

- A founder field note based on a real observation or interview.
- A 5–8 minute screen recording or talking-head lesson.
- A teardown of one anonymised message, post, or networking situation.
- A short research note with a small, transparent dataset.

From that source asset create:

- 1 LinkedIn flagship post.
- 2 LinkedIn follow-up posts or document slides.
- 3 Instagram Reels.
- 1 Instagram carousel.
- 1 X thread and 5–8 short posts.
- 5–10 thoughtful Reddit comments or one native post when a community fit exists.
- 1 email or landing-page section.

Each derivative must stand alone. Do not copy a LinkedIn post into Reddit or X unchanged.

### Example atomisation

Source: “You finished a useful project. Help the right people understand it.”

- Reel 1: turn a vague project announcement into a specific explanation of the work.
- Reel 2: show how a consultant explains a contribution without revealing client information.
- Reel 3: turn a student's project learning into a useful question for a practitioner.
- LinkedIn: show the full path from project fact to post, relevant comment, and conversation.
- X thread: compare a builder's, freelancer's, and employee's way of sharing a decision.
- Reddit: answer a relevant question about sharing work with a complete standalone example.
- Email: one work-sharing exercise and an invitation to try the free interview.

## 7. Platform execution

### Instagram

Start with three Reels and one carousel per week. The Reel should show the payoff in the first second with a visual hook, spoken hook, and text overlay. Keep the first tests between 15 and 30 seconds. Use captions that remain readable without sound.

Recurring formats:

- “Rewrite this message with me.”
- “You did the work. Here is how to explain why it matters.”
- “Share a client project without revealing anything confidential.”
- “One project, three ways to start a professional conversation.”
- “A real founder decision: why Lynkr leaves sending to the user.”
- “One question to ask before you send a referral request.”

Use one profile CTA: **“Start your free Lynkr interview.”** Supporting copy should accurately describe the currently available first output. Do not promise a post or client pitch as the free first win: the reviewed plan specifies a headline and referral message. Track profile visits, saves, shares, link taps, and interview starts. Worksheets are optional future assets, not an existing offer.

### LinkedIn

Post four times per week from the founder profile, with the company page supporting rather than leading.

- Monday: show how to make a piece of work understandable.
- Tuesday: show a useful comment, conversation opener, or follow-up.
- Thursday: demonstrate a work-to-opportunity journey for a specific audience.
- Friday: a user question, grounded communication example, or founder lesson.

Across each set of eight flagship posts, test two examples each for working professionals, freelancers/consultants, founders/builders, and students/freshers. This equal starting sample tests audience fit; it is not a permanent quota. Keep an identifiable theme of showing real work and building relationships across all examples.

Comment on 10 relevant posts each weekday only if the comments can add a real example, correction, or useful question. Do not treat comments as a volume target. LinkedIn says feed relevance reflects the content topic, recency, relationship, language, and constructive engagement, so build a clear topical identity instead of chasing unrelated reach. Posts set to “Anyone” can appear beyond first-degree connections, in search, on profiles, and in followers’ feeds. [LinkedIn relevance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724) and [LinkedIn post visibility](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a523141).

Show the real source experience, the draft, and the user's edits. Do not turn “sounds human” into a claim that AI involvement is concealed or that distribution is guaranteed.

### Reddit

Treat Reddit as a community and research surface. Create a watchlist of 8–12 communities across professional practice, freelancing, independent building, careers, and India-specific discussion. Select them for relevant questions and permitted participation, not subscriber counts. Read each community's rules and culture before posting; an appropriate community list beyond the existing career research is not yet validated.

During the initial listening phase:

- Comment helpfully on three to five relevant threads per week.
- Do not include a Lynkr link unless it directly answers the question and the community permits it.
- Publish at most one native post every two weeks, and only when it provides standalone value.
- Record the exact questions, objections, and words people use. Do not scrape profiles or collect private information.
- Reply to every response on a post for at least 48 hours.

Potential native topics:

- “What actually makes a referral request easier to answer?”
- “I analysed why generic LinkedIn comments fail. Here are the patterns.”
- “How do you share client work when most of it is confidential?”
- “How do you explain a technical project to someone outside your field?”
- “What makes a collaboration message worth answering?”

The brand should be disclosed when relevant. The account should participate as a person with a clear interest in the problem, not as a disguised ad account.

### X

Use X for idea testing and relationships with builders, freelancers, specialists, founders, and career practitioners. Start with two original posts and three thoughtful replies on active days, plus one source-asset thread per week if sustainable. Treat volumes as ceilings to adjust to capacity, not obligations.

Formats:

- One-sentence observations from user research.
- Short examples of a message before and after.
- Build decisions with the tradeoff included.
- Threads that unpack one framework from the weekly source asset.
- Replies that add an example rather than praise.

Do not automate replies, use identical copy, or attach a link to most posts. X explicitly prohibits bulk, duplicative, irrelevant, or unsolicited content and high-volume automated replies or DMs. [X Authenticity](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity) and [X automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation).

## 8. Evidence-based rollout

### Phase 1: Foundation and research

- Use the shared situation: someone has useful work to share and wants to connect with relevant people.
- Conduct eight discovery conversations, two from each audience row in §3. Ask about the last piece of work they wanted to share, whom they wanted to reach, what they tried, what felt difficult, and what happened. Capture exact language and distinguish discovery interviews from the product interview.
- Create a simple product-marketing context document from `app/CONTEXT.md`, then mark assumptions separately from verified evidence.
- Set up one landing-page CTA and one event path: profile visit → start interview → complete interview → activate paid access.
- Build the channel watchlists and a spreadsheet for content ideas, source links, format, status, and result.
- Publish the founder’s “why I am building Lynkr” post and one honest product constraint.

Exit condition: eight conversations completed, a documented trigger for each audience, one umbrella promise, one honest CTA, and baseline funnel counts. Small interview counts reveal language; they do not establish market size.

### Phase 2: Message validation

- Publish two source assets showing how real work becomes visible and supports a relevant conversation.
- Produce six Reels, two carousels, eight LinkedIn posts, two X threads, and at least ten helpful Reddit comments.
- Test three message angles: “make your work visible,” “build connections that matter,” and “open doors to opportunities.” Keep the CTA and offer consistent; rotate angles within audiences so audience and message effects are not conflated.
- Run one message teardown each week using an anonymised example.
- Ask every qualified responder which line describes their problem best.

Exit condition: record audience, angle, qualified responses, interview starts, completions, and repeat use with denominators. Treat an audience cell with fewer than 20 attributable visits or five completed product interviews as inconclusive. These are lead-chosen minimum learning thresholds, not statistical significance. Continue discovery where traffic is sparse rather than calling the largest raw count a winner.

### Phase 3: Repeat use and proof

- Keep the weekly source-asset rhythm.
- Add a recurring “Lynkr user journey” series: interview answer → confirmed fact → message/post.
- Publish one transparent mini-case study only when a real user has consented and the result is accurately stated.
- Start a weekly email or field note so social attention flows to an owned channel.
- Build a public FAQ from observed objections. Test questions about having no impressive metrics, client confidentiality, sounding generic, promoting work without bragging, and staying in control of outreach. These are prompts to investigate, not validated customer quotes.
- Test a referral invitation only after the product experience is reliable: invite one peer to complete the interview, not a generic referral discount.

Exit condition: three repeatable content formats, one owned capture loop, and at least one evidence-backed conversion story.

### Phase 4: Focus distribution from evidence

- Give more effort to formats and audience situations that produce qualified completion and repeat use. Keep showcasing real work as the strategic centre even if a job-search post attracts more impressions.
- Turn the strongest source asset into an evergreen landing page, email, Reel series, LinkedIn document, and Reddit answer bank.
- Build a 20-account relationship list across LinkedIn and X. Engage consistently and propose two collaborations only when there is a natural fit.
- Create a small “first 20 users” feedback circle. Keep it product-feedback and learning-led, not a public group that needs constant moderation.
- Review channel economics: time per qualified conversation, interview completion rate, paid activation rate, and retention signal.
- Keep all four audiences eligible. Once at least two segments meet Phase 2's learning thresholds, provisionally allocate 60% of content effort to the segment with stronger repeat-use evidence, 30% to the next, and 10% to continued exploration. If samples remain thin or results conflict, retain balanced tests. A formal community or paid experiments need separate evidence and spend authorization.

Exit condition: a documented audience/channel allocation based on qualified activation and return use, with sample sizes, uncertainty, and the next experiment recorded.

## 10. Measurement

### North-star outcome

**Activated users who return to share work or take a deliberate relationship action.** For this strategy, activation means completing the product interview and reviewing or copying the first useful output. Return use means a relevant product action in days 8–14 after activation, where the feature is available.

Keep two separate scorecards: acquisition conversations about Lynkr, and users' own professional outcomes. An acquisition conversation is qualified when the person describes a relevant need and is interested in trying the product; likes and generic compliments do not count. User outcomes include a relevant reply, peer connection, client enquiry, collaboration discussion, mentorship conversation, or career opportunity, recorded only with consent and labelled self-reported. Content published is a useful leading signal for work visibility, not proof of professional benefit.

Impressions and follower growth are diagnostic metrics. They are not the goal.

### Funnel events

1. Content view or profile visit.
2. Link click or direct question.
3. Interview start.
4. Interview completion.
5. First useful output reviewed or copied.
6. Paid activation.
7. Week-two return or repeat action.

Track channel, series, audience situation, and message angle with simple UTM parameters or a manual record. Do not include names, message text, confidential work, or LinkedIn profile URLs in analytics. Audience can be optional and self-described; do not add an onboarding question just for marketing attribution. Mark unknown sources explicitly.

Compare visit → start, start → completion, completion → first output, activation → return, and activation → paid access within matching cohorts. Evaluate return rates only after the full 14-day observation window; pending users are excluded and shown separately. Report counts alongside rates. CAC, willingness to pay by segment, and retention baselines are not found; do not infer them from engagement.

### Weekly review questions

- Which hook produced qualified questions rather than empty likes?
- Which problem language appeared in replies more than once?
- Which content caused an interview start?
- Did users complete the interview and reach the first useful output?
- Which channel produced the best qualified conversation per hour?
- Which post attracted the wrong audience or a promise Lynkr cannot keep?

## 11. Risks and guardrails

- **Overpromising outcomes:** promise help showcasing work and building relationships; never guaranteed jobs, clients, reach, or revenue.
- **Audience dilution:** broad brand, specific examples. Each asset names one situation and one next step rather than listing every persona.
- **Product/positioning mismatch:** the reviewed first win is still referral-oriented and onboarding names fresher, experienced job seeker, and networker buckets. Log friction from broader users. Do not advertise dedicated freelancer onboarding or a free client pitch until built and verified; any implementation amendment is separate work.
- **AI distrust:** show the user’s evidence and review step; never present invented results.
- **Reddit or X spam risk:** use human participation, community-specific context, low link frequency, and no bulk automation.
- **Channel sprawl:** one source asset each week keeps production manageable. Cut channels when they consume time without qualified activation.
- **Weak proof:** label hypotheses and anecdotes. Do not turn one user story into a market claim.
- **Privacy:** anonymise examples and never collect private profile data or passwords.
- **Founder burnout:** batch content, leave room for live replies, and define a sustainable weekly time budget before increasing volume.

## 12. Decisions and remaining unknowns

### Decided by Aryan

- Purpose: help people market themselves and their work, build good connections, and find opportunities.
- Audience: broader than freshers; freshers remain included.

### Decided by the lead (reversible)

- Use the umbrella promise and four audience situations above; start with work-sharing as the common situation.
- Use the founder profile as the main voice, with the company account supporting it.
- Keep one conversion CTA to the free interview and describe its actual first output accurately.
- Keep existing price and product scope; use a starting content mix of 40/30/20/10 and the learning thresholds in §8.
- Start with LinkedIn and one reusable source asset. Add Instagram, Reddit, X, and email activity only within demonstrated capacity; the asset menu is not a requirement to publish every derivative.

### Still unknown; investigate without blocking this revision

1. Sustainable production and reply capacity.
2. Verified current paying-user counts, completions, testimonials, and consented outcomes.
3. Which audience situations produce sustained use and willingness to pay.
4. Whether current onboarding and the referral-oriented first win serve non-job-seekers well.
5. International demand, language needs, and billing suitability.
6. Whether an owned email channel can be maintained; use the existing site until then.

Until these are answered, treat the plan as a disciplined test programme, not a forecast.
